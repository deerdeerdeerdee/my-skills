# Trade Data Screening

Use this reference when import/export statistics will affect product-market selection. The goal is a comparable market screen, not a buyer list, forecast, tariff ruling, or profit estimate.

## Define product codes

Describe material, function, form and intended use before selecting codes. Propose no more than three plausible HS6 candidates and document why each may fit. Verify heading text, section/chapter notes and exclusions in a current customs nomenclature source. National tariff lines can subdivide HS6 differently; retain ambiguity when the product cannot be classified confidently. Do not choose a code only because its trade value looks large.

## Query comparable data

Use an official or clearly documented source that exposes its query definition. Common starting points include [UN Comtrade](https://comtrade.un.org/) and [ITC Trade Map](https://www.trademap.org/Index.aspx); use the destination customs authority for current tariff lines and rates. For a given reporter and code, record:

- Import flow, reporter market and partner/origin, usually both World and China.
- A consistent 3-5 year period, source release date, currency/value basis, net weight, supplementary quantity and unit.
- Whether values are reporter or mirror data, reported or estimated, suppressed, revised, or missing.
- HS revision and national-code differences, query parameters, retrieval date and source URL.

Compare value and quantity trends only when the year, code, reporter/partner and measurement unit are comparable. Do not calculate a trend across incompatible code revisions or mixed quantity units. Unit values are customs-value ratios, not retail prices, supplier quotes or attainable margins.

## Separate market size from buying evidence

Trade statistics can indicate imports, source-country composition and broad market movement. They do not identify the specific companies that buy, prove an individual lead imports, establish current demand, or show that Chinese supply is competitive. Use a separate discovery path such as local wholesale/retail directories, buyer RFQs, trade-show lists, project notices, importer records where lawful and sufficiently documented, or Google Maps plus business-source corroboration. Keep the discovery channel, validation sources and eventual contact route in separate fields.

Check tariffs, preferential treatment, additional duties, product controls and importer-of-record responsibility in the destination's current official tariff/regulatory sources for the specific country, product description and origin. A trade database's HS classification or tariff estimate is a starting point, not a transaction-date compliance check.

## Decision record

For each candidate market, state the code and classification confidence, import value/quantity and time trend, China share when comparable, major data caveats, current official tariff source/date, local substitutes/channels, and the next evidence needed from actual companies. If classification, unit comparability, coverage or data provenance prevents ranking, report that the data cannot rank the markets. Never convert a macro import value into an estimated sales opportunity without explicit, defensible assumptions.
